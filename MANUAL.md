## Overview

MFS Assets is asset management inside FreeScout. You register laptops, phones, monitors, printers, network gear and software licences, and for each one you answer three questions: **whose is it** (the owner, an organisation), **who uses it** (a person, a department or nobody) and **where is it** (a location). When a customer writes in, the agent sees that person's equipment next to the conversation and links the right asset in one click.

It works the same for every kind of team: an IT helpdesk that manages its own company's equipment, a service provider that manages its clients' equipment, and a provider whose own router sits at a client. Your own company is simply one organisation in the list.

What you get:

- An **Assets** page in the top menu: a list with search, filters and sorting, and a page per asset with its details, history, conversations and files.
- **Asset types** with their own fields (text, number, date, choice list, yes/no), for example RAM for laptops or the IMEI for phones.
- An **Assets block** on every conversation: the customer's own assets, their department's and the shared ones, linked with one click.
- An **Assets** tab on the customer page and on the organisation page.
- Search by asset tag or serial number, and the tags of linked assets in the conversation list.
- A full **history**: who changed what, when an asset changed hands or moved.
- **QR labels** with a report form for people without a FreeScout login.
- **CSV import and export**, a **REST API** and **webhooks**.

**Requirements:** FreeScout 1.8 or newer and **MFS Organisations 1.1 or newer** (owners, departments and locations come from there). For the API and webhooks you also need FreeScout's official API & Webhooks module; the badges on Kanban cards need the Kanban module.

## Installation and licence

Install MFS Organisations 1.1 or newer first. Then install MFS Assets like any other FreeScout module:

1. Download the zip from your customer account (or from the help center) and unpack it into FreeScout's `Modules` folder, so you get `Modules/MFSAssets`.
2. In FreeScout go to **Manage → Modules** and click **Activate** at MFS Assets. FreeScout creates the tables.
3. Go to **Manage → Settings → MFS Assets**, paste your licence key and click **Activate licence**.
4. On the same page choose **Our organisation** (your own company in MFS Organisations) and click **Save**.

The licence is checked every 6 hours. Without a valid licence MFS Assets is switched off: the Assets menu and blocks disappear, but your assets, history and files stay stored and are back as soon as the licence is active again.

**Updates** appear under **Manage → Modules** with an **Update** button; your data is kept.

**Who can do what**

| Who | Can |
| --- | --- |
| Every agent | See the assets of the organisations they may see (through the organisation's mailboxes), link and unlink assets on conversations, print QR labels |
| Agents with "Users are allowed to manage assets" | Also create, edit and import assets and upload files |
| Administrators | Everything, plus the settings, asset types and deleting assets |

You give the permission per agent under **Manage → Users → (agent) → Permissions**.

## Settings and asset types

Administrators find the settings under **Manage → Settings → MFS Assets**.

| Setting | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Our organisation | Your own company in MFS Organisations. New assets get it as owner unless you choose a client. |
| Asset tag prefix | New assets are numbered with this prefix: AST-0001, AST-0002 and so on. Changing it only affects new assets. |
| Report form mailbox | Reports sent through a QR label become conversations in this mailbox. Leave it empty to show no form (the label then only says the item is registered). |

![Manage → Settings → MFS Assets: licence, our organisation, tag prefix and report form mailbox](https://managedfreescout.com/wp-content/uploads/manuals/mfsassets/12-settings.png?v=92c22323)

**Asset types.** Under **Assets → Types** an administrator adds types such as Laptop, Phone, Monitor or Software licence. Each type can have its own fields, one per line, in the form `Label | type | options`:

```
RAM | select | 8 GB, 16 GB, 32 GB
Operating system | text
Encrypted | checkbox
Warranty contract | date
Seats | number
```

The field types are `text`, `number`, `date`, `select` (a choice list; the options follow after the second `|`, separated by commas) and `checkbox` (yes/no). The fields appear on the asset form and the asset page as soon as an asset has that type. A field is recognised by its label: changing the label or removing the line hides the old values (they stay stored, and come back if you restore the label). A type that is still in use cannot be deleted.

![Asset types and their own fields](https://managedfreescout.com/wp-content/uploads/manuals/mfsassets/04-types.png?v=fb8daa96)

## Registering assets

Click **Assets** in the top menu. The list shows every asset you may see, with its tag, name and serial number, type, status, owner, who uses it, where it is and the end of the warranty (red when it has passed). Search by tag, name, serial number or e-mail address, filter by type, owner and status, and click a column header to sort. Retired and disposed assets are hidden unless you choose them in the status filter.

![The Assets list with search, filters and sorting](https://managedfreescout.com/wp-content/uploads/manuals/mfsassets/01-list.png?v=d6246a4b)

**New asset** opens the form. It has three parts:

| Part | Fields |
| --- | --- |
| The asset | Name, type, status |
| Whose is it, who uses it, where is it | **Owner**: an organisation (default: your own). **Assigned to**: a person (e-mail address), a department of the owner, or nobody (shared, on purpose). **Location**: a location of the owner or of any other organisation you can see, or none (for example for a software licence) |
| Details | Serial number, supplier, purchase date and price, warranty end, the type's own fields, notes |

The tag (AST-0001) is given automatically when you save. Departments and locations are kept in MFS Organisations: an organisation's **Departments** and **Locations** tabs.

![The asset form: whose is it, who uses it, where is it, details and the type's own fields](https://managedfreescout.com/wp-content/uploads/manuals/mfsassets/03-edit-form.png?v=8f21ac21)

The status follows the asset's life:

| Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Ordered | Bought, not delivered yet |
| In stock | Delivered, not in use |
| In use | Somebody or something uses it |
| In repair | Temporarily away |
| Retired | No longer used, still owned |
| Disposed | Gone (sold, recycled, lost) |

**The asset page** shows all details, the conversations it is linked to and the history: when it was created, every change of hands (assigned), every move (location), every status change and any other change, with who did it and when. Under **Files** you keep the invoice, warranty card or a photo (up to 15 MB per file; files are stored privately and only agents who may see the asset can download them).

![An asset page with its details, conversations, history and files](https://managedfreescout.com/wp-content/uploads/manuals/mfsassets/02-asset-page.png?v=57e6ab31)

To stop using an asset, set it to **Retired** or **Disposed**: it keeps its history and conversations. Only an administrator can delete an asset, and deleting removes its history and files for good.

## Assets in conversations

**The Assets block.** In every conversation the right-hand panel shows an **Assets** block, under the organisation block of MFS Organisations:

- First the assets already linked to this conversation; **×** unlinks one.
- Then suggestions, grouped as **Of this person** (assigned to the customer's e-mail address), **Of their department** and **Shared in the organisation** (owned by the conversation's organisation, assigned to nobody). Click **Link** to link one.
- A search box: type a tag, name or serial number and pick the asset to link it, also when it isn't among the suggestions.
- **New asset for this conversation** opens the form with the owner and the person already filled in; after saving, the new asset is linked and you are back in the conversation.

![The Assets block on a conversation, under the organisation block](https://managedfreescout.com/wp-content/uploads/manuals/mfsassets/05-conversation.png?v=35337e0c)

Linking is logged in the asset's history, so the asset page shows every conversation about it.

**Customer and organisation pages.** The customer page has an **Assets** tab with everything assigned to any of the customer's e-mail addresses. The organisation page in MFS Organisations has an **Assets** tab too: the assets the organisation owns, and below them the assets of others at its locations (for example your router at a client's office).

![The Assets tab on a customer page](https://managedfreescout.com/wp-content/uploads/manuals/mfsassets/06-customer-tab.png?v=8a4c2fd5)

![The Assets tab on an organisation page, with others' assets at its locations](https://managedfreescout.com/wp-content/uploads/manuals/mfsassets/07-organisation-tab.png?v=480be220)

**Search.** In FreeScout's search, type a tag or serial number (AST-0012, 7HQ2MZ3) to find the conversations linked to that asset. Or add the filter **Asset** and enter the tag or serial number there.

![FreeScout's search with the Asset filter](https://managedfreescout.com/wp-content/uploads/manuals/mfsassets/08-search-filter.png?v=2d87f653)

**Badges.** In the conversation list and in search results, the tags of the linked assets appear after the subject (at most two, then +n). With the Kanban module they also appear on the cards.

![Asset tags after the subject in the conversation list](https://managedfreescout.com/wp-content/uploads/manuals/mfsassets/09-list-badges.png?v=4cb17ebb)

## QR labels and the report form

On an asset page, **QR label** opens a printable label of 62 × 29 mm (the size of common Brother and DYMO label printers) with a QR code, the tag and the name. Click **Print** and stick it on the device.

![A QR label of 62 × 29 mm](https://managedfreescout.com/wp-content/uploads/manuals/mfsassets/10-label.png?v=aa5aa780)

The QR code holds a random link (`/a/…`), never the asset number, so nobody can guess the links of other assets. What happens when someone scans it:

- **An agent who is logged in** and may see the asset goes straight to the asset page.
- **Anyone else** gets a short report form: name, e-mail address and what the problem is. Sending it creates a conversation in the report form mailbox (see Settings), from that person, already linked to the asset and logged in its history as "Reported via QR label". The person gets an answer by e-mail like any other customer.

![The report form on a phone](https://managedfreescout.com/wp-content/uploads/manuals/mfsassets/11-report-form.png?v=f4aed99f)

The form shows only the tag and the name of the asset, never its owner, user or location. It is protected against abuse: at most 5 reports per hour from the same connection, and a hidden field that catches spam robots. Without a report form mailbox the page only says the item is registered.

## CSV import and export, privacy

**Export CSV** on the Assets page downloads the list as you filtered it. The first line holds the column names:

```
tag,name,type,status,owner,assigned_email,assigned_department,location,serial,supplier,purchase_date,purchase_price,warranty_end,notes,fields
```

The last column holds the type's own fields as JSON.

**Import CSV** (agents who may manage assets) reads a file with the same columns. Only `name` is required; columns you leave out are not changed. Commas and semicolons both work.

![Import CSV on the Assets page](https://managedfreescout.com/wp-content/uploads/manuals/mfsassets/13-import.png?v=d132d29d)

- An empty `tag` creates a new asset; an existing tag updates that asset.
- `owner`: the organisation's name (empty = your own organisation). `type`: an existing type name. `assigned_department`: a department name of the owner. `location`: the path as shown in FreeScout, for example `Head office › Building A` (`>` works too).
- `status`: ordered, in\_stock, in\_use, in\_repair, retired or disposed. Dates as 2026-11-08.
- The type's own fields are not imported.

```
tag;name;type;owner;assigned_email;location;status;serial;supplier
;ThinkPad T14;Laptop;Acme;liam.patel@example.com;Head office > Building A;in_use;PF4XK2LM;Lenovo
AST-0005;Brother MFC – 2nd floor;Printer;Acme;;Head office > Building A;in_repair;;
```

After the import you see how many assets were created and updated; lines with a problem (an unknown owner, tag or location) are listed by line number and skipped.

**Privacy (GDPR).** Assets are linked to people by e-mail address only. Deleting a customer in FreeScout unlinks that person's assets (they become shared and the history says why); the assets themselves are never deleted. For a request to see someone's data, open the customer's **Assets** tab and click **Export CSV**: you get every asset assigned to that address, retired ones included.

## API and webhooks

With FreeScout's official **API & Webhooks** module installed, MFS Assets adds:

- A REST API under `/api/assets`: list (with filters and paging), read (optionally with history and conversations), create, change, delete, link and unlink conversations, and the asset types. It uses the same API keys as FreeScout's API; a personal API key sees and changes only what its agent may.
- Eight webhook events: `asset.created`, `asset.updated`, `asset.deleted`, `asset.assigned`, `asset.moved`, `asset.status_changed`, `asset.linked` and `asset.unlinked`. Choose them in the API & Webhooks module's webhook settings. Conversation webhooks also get an `assets` field with the linked assets.

The API works only with an active licence. All endpoints, fields and examples: [MFS Assets — API and webhooks](https://managedfreescout.com/support/mfsassets-api/).
